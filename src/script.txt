In 1978, Haruki Murakami was watching a baseball game, beer in hand, when a batter hit a double and a thought drifted in: I could try writing a novel. David Brooks says every calling begins this way, with a small moment of enchantment that you didn't choose.

Then comes curiosity, the kind that keeps you up at night, wanting to know everything.

Soon after, you notice the gap between how good you are and how good you'd like to be, and oddly, that gap is what pulls you forward.

Close it a little, and you get your first taste of mastery, until you find you love the long hours as much as the result.

And that is what carries you through the pain, the way it carried Murakami through twenty-three marathons.

Because the real prize was never the finish line. It's the person you become on the way there.
